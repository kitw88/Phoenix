Allowed labels: AQ, DQ, CFF, Sharkfin, ELN, Step-down FCN, FCN, Step-down SCN, Phoenix, WRA, BEN, DCN, outside.

Apply the first matching rule and stop.

1. AQ — Accumulator.
OTC derivative, not a note. The investor buys a specified amount of the underlying at the strike on a series of predetermined dates before the contract terminates. The underlying may be a stock, an FX pair, or a precious metal such as gold. Early knock-out, including an American knock-out, stops later purchases. A larger purchase when the fixing is below the strike is still AQ. A guaranteed fixing period is still AQ. One delivery of the underlying at a note's maturity is not AQ.

2. DQ — Decumulator.
Same contract and same underlyings as AQ, except the investor sells the specified amount at the strike on those dates. Early knock-out stops later sales.

3. CFF — Cap Floor Floater.
The investor receives enhanced periodic coupons linked to an interest rate. The Floor Rate secures a higher minimum coupon. Any upside beyond the Cap Rate is given up. Held to maturity, the note usually returns 100% of principal. This is the principal-protected rate product. An equity knock-out level is not a Cap Rate, and a coupon barrier on a stock is not a Floor Rate.

4. Sharkfin — SharkFin.
Principal is generally protected, so a poor underlying performance still returns the initial investment. The return increases when the underlying moves in the anticipated direction, up to a predefined cap or price ceiling. The direction is bullish, bearish, or twin-win, and twin-win means both sides can benefit the investor. Some SharkFin products also knock out: if the underlying reaches a barrier, the product settles early and the return can be capped. A knock-out is not required. Bullish, bearish, and twin-win all use the label Sharkfin. A reverse-convertible autocall is not a Sharkfin, because that note can settle below par.

5. Reverse-convertible gate.
If the contract is not AQ, DQ, CFF, or Sharkfin, continue only when the instrument is a note that is not principal protected and a downside condition can settle it in shares or in cash worth less than par. Otherwise the label is outside.

6. ELN.
All of the following: reverse convertible; issued at a discount; the yield is per annum; no coupon is paid during the life; the investor receives 100% of notional if the stock closes at or above the strike on the final observation date. A note issued at 100% with coupons during the life is not an ELN, even if the title says Equity Linked Note.

7. Per-annum coupon family.
The coupon is per annum when the amount grows with the time invested: an annualized rate, a per-period fraction of a yearly rate, or a fixed percent of notional on each observation date. A knock-out coupon equal to an annualized rate multiplied by the time elapsed is included. The annualized rate may change during the life, including a step down to a lower annual rate, so a later knock-out date can show a smaller coupon than the previous date. The amount may be paid on scheduled dates during the life, or in one sum on knock-out or at maturity. All five names below are reverse convertibles. Within this family, use the first matching bullet.

- WRA: the coupon accrues each day only if the worst-performing underlying, the laggard, is at or above its own coupon accrual barrier that day.
- Step-down FCN: each scheduled coupon is unconditional, with no coupon-barrier test, and the autocall or knock-out level declines across successive observation dates.
- FCN: each scheduled coupon is unconditional, and the knock-out level does not decline. A knock-in barrier or strike below the initial price is not a step-down.
- Step-down SCN: the knock-out coupon accrues as an annualized rate multiplied by the time elapsed. The annualized rate may step down partway through the term. A knock-out, when the underlying closes at or above the knock-out barrier, terminates the note and pays 100% of notional plus the accrued coupon for that date. The knock-out barrier may stay constant or decline; both use the label Step-down SCN. If knock-out never occurs, either maturity result is still Step-down SCN: 100% of notional when the final close is above the strike and below the knock-out barrier, or 100% of notional plus the final accrued coupon when no knock-in or barrier event has occurred. After a knock-in, settlement below the strike can be cash below par or physical delivery at the strike.
- Phoenix: a coupon date can pay its coupon while the note continues, because the coupon trigger and the knock-out are not the same event. The coupon barrier is separate from the autocall or knock-out level. Use Phoenix whether a missed coupon is paid later or forfeited. Remembering that an underlying has hit an autocall trigger is an autocall memory, not a Phoenix memory coupon.

8. Flat payoff family.
The coupon or bonus is one absolute amount. An early knock-out and a later knock-out pay that same amount. A knock-out coupon that accrues with an annualized rate, including a rate that steps down during the term, is Step-down SCN. A maturity redemption of 100% of notional plus that final accrued coupon is Step-down SCN. Both names below are reverse convertibles.

- BEN: at maturity the investor receives the higher of a Bonus Coupon and the positive performance of the worst-performing underlying in the basket.
- DCN: at maturity one fixed coupon amount is paid if the coupon condition is fulfilled, and that amount is the same whichever knock-out date is used. The payoff is not the higher of a bonus and the worst performer's upside.

9. If none of the labels fit, return outside.
